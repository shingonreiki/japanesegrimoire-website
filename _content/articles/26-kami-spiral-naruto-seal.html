If you've watched Naruto, you know the spiral. Whenever Naruto draws on the power of the Nine-Tails, a swirl appears around his navel, and the whole story turns on whether that seal holds. What most fans don't know is that the spiral has a much older life in Japan, right inside the character for a divine spirit, kami (神). I've seen it carved in stone more than once, and it says something about seals that the anime gets surprisingly right.

A kami written as a spiral

The character 神 has two parts. On the left is the altar radical (示, squeezed into 礻), which shows up in many characters that have to do with the sacred and with offerings. On the right is 申. Many scholars of early Chinese script read this part as a picture of lightning, the flash that comes down from the sky. Put simply, the character shows the altar and the power that comes down to it.

In everyday writing, 神 looks the way you'd find it in any dictionary. But in Japan there are special ways of writing it, and in some of them the right-hand part is drawn as a spiral. I've come across these several times, for example on stone steles at Fushimi Inari in Kyoto, where thousands of small stone mounds and altars line the paths up the mountain. My own take is that the spiral carries a meaning here. I read it as a hint that the power of a spirit is called in through the spiral.

That fits the way spirals show up elsewhere in Japanese sacred spaces. Look at the round roof tiles of a shrine and you'll often find the tomoe (巴), a swirl of one, two or three comma shapes, and you'll find similar swirls on the big shrine drums. A turning shape is what energy looks like when it gathers, a bit like water in a whirlpool.

The seal on Naruto's belly

Now to the anime. On the night the Nine-Tails attacked the village, Naruto's father, Minato, sealed the power of the Nine-Tails inside his newborn son. The seal he used is called the Eight Trigrams Seal, Hakke Fūin (八卦封印). Hakke are the eight trigrams of the old Chinese Book of Changes, the same eight signs you'll find in Daoist cosmology and in Japanese onmyōdō. So even the name reaches into a real tradition.

What I find interesting is how the story builds this seal. The spiral is only what you see. In the manga, the seal is made of two Four Symbols Seals laid on top of each other, a structure that lets a little of the fox's chakra flow to Naruto while holding the rest back. There's a key to it, which Jiraiya keeps. And when Orochimaru wants to weaken Naruto, he presses a Five Elements Seal onto it, an odd number laid over an even one, and the whole thing goes out of balance until Jiraiya removes it again.

In other words, the spiral by itself doesn't hold anything. It holds because of everything that was set up around it.

Why a spiral alone isn't enough

This is where the anime touches something real. My view is that the spiral calls the power and gathers it, but it doesn't keep it stable on its own. For that it needs a ritual counterpart, something that gives the power its frame and its direction. In Japanese sacred practice a sign rarely stands by itself, and that's exactly why.

How that's done in practice isn't something for a blog post, and I won't try to turn it into a recipe here. It belongs in a setting where it can be passed on properly.

The spiral in Reiki

If you practice Reiki, you've met this spiral already. The first Reiki symbol, Cho Ku Rei, has a spiral at its heart. It's usually called the power symbol, and that's not wrong. Its name, though, contains rei (靈), the spiritual, and in my view the symbol brings that spiritual power into the here and now, very focused, in this exact moment. That's where its amplifying effect comes from.

And Cho Ku Rei is exactly the kind of counterpart I mean. It's combined with the other symbols because it gives them their direction. With Sei He Ki, it sets where that symbol's spiritual power should work. With distance Reiki, the distance symbol alone isn't enough to make the connection, and Cho Ku Rei fixes the direction to the person. I've written more about the name and the spiral in the article on Cho Ku Rei on shingon-reiki.com, and there's an overview of all four in the article on the Reiki symbols.

Where to look for spirals in Japan

At Fushimi Inari, take your time on the upper paths of Mount Inari, where the stone mounds and altars stand close together, and look at how the characters are carved. I write more about the shrine itself in the article on Fushimi Inari. At almost any larger shrine, look up at the ends of the roof tiles for the tomoe.
