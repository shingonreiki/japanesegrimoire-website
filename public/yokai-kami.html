Yokai & Kami

The spirits of Japan, from the nine-tailed fox to the long-nosed tengu, and what they really meant to the people who told their stories.

Japan has a word for the strange things that happen at the edge of the village: yōkai (妖怪). And it has a word for the powers people worship at shrines: kami (神). The line between the two isn't always clear. A fox can be a trickster in one story and the messenger of a kami in the next, and the tengu of the mountains started out as dangerous beings and ended up as protectors.

In this section I go through the spirits one by one. Where did the stories come from? How did the old picture scrolls and woodblock masters show them? And where can you still see them in Japan today, carved in stone at a shrine, painted on a festival mask, or sold as a little charm?

The illustrations on these pages are original artwork that follows the traditional Japanese ways of depicting each being.
