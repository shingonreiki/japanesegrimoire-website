Sacred Places

Shrines, temples, waterfalls and mountains, and why the Japanese have treated them as sacred for over a thousand years.

Most travel guides tell you how to get to a shrine, when it opens and where to buy the best snacks nearby. That's useful. This section looks at the same places from another angle: why they became sacred, which kami or buddhas are present there, and what people have done there for centuries.

I lived in Kyoto for about three years and spent a lot of that time in temples. I also walked the full Shikoku pilgrimage on foot, all 88 temples, after giving up at Temple 24 on my first try. So some of these places I know quite well, including the parts where your feet hurt.

The places mentioned in the other sections show up here too, Mount Kurama with its tengu, or Fushimi Inari with its foxes, so this section will grow as the rest of the grimoire grows.
