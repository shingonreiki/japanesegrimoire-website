Kuji Kiri & Japanese Hand Seals

You've seen the hand signs in Naruto and Jujutsu Kaisen. Here's the tradition they came from.

Nine characters, 臨兵闘者皆陣列在前, nine hand positions and nine short mantras. That is the outer shape of Kuji Kiri, the practice of the nine seals. Most people in the West meet it on a screen first, usually as a ninja trick. In Japan it has a much longer and much stranger history.

The earliest sources come from China. Over the centuries the material reached Japan in several waves, through the constant exchange with the mainland, and Japan kept working on it for centuries more. What came out of that is a system you only find in Japan: a combination of Daoism, Japanese folk magic and the esoteric Buddhism of Shingon (mikkyō), carried along by the mountain ascetics of Shugendō and closely tied to Shintō.

In this section I write about that history, about the mudras of esoteric Buddhism, and about the hand signs you know from anime and what they actually point to. You won't find how-to instructions here. The practice itself is passed on in person, and that's where it belongs.
